01 · The website

Measure first, then build.

We pulled the baseline before writing a line of code, so every later number has something to be compared against.

01

The baseline said the site was invisible

31 organic keywords. 58 visits a month. $0 in traffic value. No paid search at all. And 96.55% of that organic traffic came from one term — the firm's own name. For anyone who did not already know the firm by name, it did not exist.

Source: Semrush, live domain, 31 Jul 2026
02

Two local competitors were 12× and 32× bigger

The firm drew 58 visits a month. Its two nearest competitors drew 686 and 1,856. The gap was not expertise. It was published answers — the competitors had pages for the questions people type, and the firm did not.

Source: Semrush organic traffic, same pull
03

One page was already earning, and it was a tool

Their 401(k) benchmarking page held real commercial rankings — six keywords at $3.85 to $9.95 CPC, positions 44 to 82. Not an article. A calculator. That finding changed what we build first.

Source: Semrush keyword table, /benchmarking
04

The cutover was engineered, not hoped for

The old site had roughly 93 live URLs — 75 blog posts, nine pages, five categories, three event pages, one booking page. Every one is mapped to a redirect. Without that map, launch day throws away whatever the firm had already earned.

05

Compliance runs as a script, not a memory

A gate runs before every deploy. It reads every visible string, every JSON-LD value, and the answer-engine file, and it fails the build if wording drifts off what a fee-based fiduciary may claim. At the second firm we ran the same idea by hand: an SEC Marketing Rule and NAIC sweep that corrected "fee-only" to "fee-based" site-wide and put the required disclosure on every page and every email.

02 · Paid advertising

Cost per lead is the only score.

Meta campaigns for the second firm's webinars and in-person workshops. These are our own results on our own account, not category averages.

Campaign typeCost per leadWhat we learned
Federal webinar ads$11–21Speaker credentials in the headline beat every other angle
In-person workshop, v1~$29Restricted targeting setting was on — it cut performance roughly in half
In-person workshop, rebuiltunder $25Same offer, correct targeting, three creative angles per geofence

The unglamorous part is where the money was. Specific dollar figures in ad copy got more clicks and worse cost per lead — and they are a compliance problem. We cut them. One targeting toggle was worth more than every creative test we ran.

03 · Marketing tracking

Every lead names the ad that made it.

Attribution is built into the form, not bolted on after. The founder opens one tab and sees the whole picture.

01

The form carries the campaign

Every landing page holds a hidden campaign ID. The submit handler writes the Salesforce lead, sets the channel, and attaches the person to that campaign automatically. No one types a source by hand, so no one forgets.

02

One dashboard, five channels

Leads today by channel. Leads this week by channel. Volume over 30 days. Federal versus general. Lead source over 90 days. Today's leads as a table. It runs as a full-org user, so the owner sees every lead, not just his own.

03

Spend syncs itself, so ROI is a real number

Facebook ad spend syncs into the campaign record automatically. Cost per lead is then computed in the CRM — actual cost divided by leads — and carried through to appointment, meeting, and closed-won.

04

Brand awareness sits beside the pipeline

A GA4 view for sessions, pages, and referral sources is embedded next to the lead dashboard, so the top of the funnel and the bottom are read in one place.

05

Every funnel is drawn end to end

Channel → landing page → CRM lead → nurture → advisor call → close, mapped for the webinar funnel, the event funnel, and the replay funnel. When a number looks wrong, we can point at the step that broke instead of guessing.

06

We found what was not instrumented, and said so

The first firm's site carried 31 phone links and 25 email links with no tracking of any kind — no analytics, no pixel, no tag. We reported that before we quoted anything. Analytics now ships with the site rather than after it.

04 · Content

One page per discipline. One page per situation.

Generic "wealth management" pages do not rank and do not convert. Both firms got pages written for a single decision a single person is facing.

Firm A

Built for the moment money changes hands

Situations: sold a business · big income year · equity compensation windfall · inherited wealth · federal employee retirement.

Insights: the Roth conversion window · concentrated stock · the first year after you sell · what a 3(38) fiduciary actually does · tax planning as a season · ten questions to ask before you hire an advisor.

Plus: a 401(k) employer page, a state-level retirement-planning page, a what-to-bring page for first meetings, and a referral program page.

Firm B

Built for one specialty, taught live

Webinar series: a recurring federal education program — FERS pension, TSP, VERA/VSIP/RIF, FEHB and Medicare open season. Each session gets a registration page with event schema, a calendar file, a confirmation and reminder sequence, a replay page, and a recap email.

Depth assets: a 13-guide hub, a self-directed brokerage section with an 18-question FAQ, and federal calculators including FERS and severance.

In person: workshops run at university and museum venues near the agencies we serve, promoted by geofenced ads, billboards, and sponsored articles — each with its own tracked source.

The pattern that works: a tool out-earns an article, and a taught session out-earns a tool. The first firm's single best-ranking page was a calculator. The second firm's strongest channel is a recurring class where the advisor teaches for an hour and never pitches.

05 · Reading this honestly

What we are not claiming.

—

The rebuilt site has not launched yet

The site is built and the redirect map is written, and you can walk through it above. The domain still points at the old site, because the cutover is the client's call. So there is no traffic lift to report, and we will not invent one.

—

Every number here is a measurement or our own ad history

The traffic figures come from a named tool on a named date. The cost-per-lead figures come from our own ad account. Nothing on this page is a forecast.

—

Neither firm publishes client testimonials

One of them does not use them at all, by policy. We build trust with credentials, disclosures, and published thinking instead. If that is your posture too, the whole approach transfers.

What this looks like for a national practice

The local firms win on city pages. A national practice wins on discipline pages and a teaching engine — the same build, aimed at a specialty instead of a map. The first step is the same either way: pull the real baseline, so the second month can be compared to the first.
